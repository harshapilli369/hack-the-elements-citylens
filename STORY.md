# Chain Reaction — Project Story

## The Problem Statement

**When regions experience sudden large-scale population influx due to conflict, climate disasters, or economic collapse, destination environments undergo rapid multi-system degradation — spanning land use, freshwater availability, and air quality — that existing disaster response and environmental monitoring systems fail to capture in an integrated, predictive way, leaving planners unable to anticipate or prevent cascading secondary crises.**

Current climate and urban planning systems treat population movement and environmental monitoring as separate domains. While tools exist to predict natural disasters or model urban growth, none integrate population shock events with real-time environmental stress indicators across land, water, and air systems simultaneously.

**Three core failures of existing solutions:**

1. **Reactive** — environmental damage is assessed months after it becomes irreversible
2. **Fragmented** — migration data, satellite land-use data, water stress indices, and air quality monitoring exist in completely separate pipelines with no connective layer
3. **Single-outcome focused** — no current tool models the feedback loop where environmental degradation at destination regions drives secondary displacement, creating cascading crises across multiple regions over time

This leaves city planners, humanitarian organizations, and governments with no proactive tool to anticipate or mitigate the chain reactions triggered by rapid, large-scale population concentration.

---

## What Is Actually Happening

When people are forced to move in large numbers — due to wildfire, flood, conflict, drought, or economic collapse — they concentrate in nearby "safer" cities. These destination areas are rarely designed to handle sudden population surges.

Multiple systems in the receiving city get stressed simultaneously:

| System | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Land** | Informal settlements, urban sprawl, green space loss |
| **Water** | Groundwater depletion, strained municipal supply |
| **Waste** | Overflow, contamination of water systems |
| **Air** | Higher traffic, construction, energy demand |
| **Health infrastructure** | Hospitals, schools, transport systems overwhelmed |

These stresses don't happen independently — **they reinforce each other.** Overcrowding increases waste, which contaminates water, which strains public health, which increases infrastructure demand. No existing tool shows this feedback loop in real time.

---

## Real Evidence

- **Fort McMurray 2016**: 88,000 people displaced by wildfire. Calgary and Edmonton received the majority. Neither city had any ecological impact assessment of the influx — only humanitarian capacity counts.
- **California Camp Fire 2018**: 50,000 displaced to Chico and Sacramento. Chico's homelessness tripled within 18 months. Water demand exceeded watershed capacity. None of this was in any disaster plan.
- **Alberta → Ontario economic migration (2023–2024)**: 40,000+ net interprovincial migrants. Statistics Canada tracks the headcount. Nobody tracks the ecological cost — the 2,000+ ha of habitat converted, the watershed stress added, the CO₂ footprint shift.
- **Phoenix water crisis**: The Colorado River is functionally depleted after decades of Sunbelt population growth. Every climate model predicted it. No urban planning tool connected incoming people to outgoing river capacity in real time.

**The pattern is consistent:** the disaster gets modeled; the displacement gets counted; the ecological chain reaction at the destination gets noticed 20 years too late.

## Who Is Affected

- **Municipal emergency managers** — planning receiving-city capacity before a disaster, not after
- **Federal climate adaptation offices** — mapping where ecological damage accumulates as climate migration grows
- **UN humanitarian agencies (UNHCR / UNDP)** — modeling cascade risk in climate-vulnerable regions
- **Infrastructure investors and insurers** — pricing receiving-end risk that current models cannot see
- **Urban and environmental planners** — understanding the ecological cost of being a destination city before it's irreversible

## Current Solution Gaps

| What existing tools do | What is missing |
|---|---|
| Model hazard at the origin | Model what the destination absorbs |
| Count people displaced | Show multi-system ecological stress at receiving cities |
| Static maps of at-risk zones | Live cascade through a connected city network |
| IPCC projections of uninhabitable zones | What happens to ecosystems where people land |
| Separate: migration data / land-use / water indices | Integrated: one view of the chain reaction |

## Our Solution — Chain Reaction

Chain Reaction is the first tool that integrates population displacement events with multi-system ecological impact modeling across land (Earth), freshwater (Water), and atmosphere (Air).

### Two Layers, One Story

**Layer 1 — CityFlow Simulator (Visual)**

Watch a disaster strike a city. Watch people move along migration corridors. Watch the receiving city's land, water, waste, air, and health systems respond in real time. Adjust the type of disaster, the speed, the migration pressure. Understand the chain reaction visually before any numbers appear.

Each fictional city maps to a real Canadian province archetype — Alberta (wildfire/drought), British Columbia (flood/coastal), Ontario (primary receiver), Saskatchewan (drought/prairie) — so every scenario reflects a plausible Canadian displacement event.

**Layer 2 — Ecological Model (Quantitative)**

After the chain reaction plays out visually, a single click pre-fills the Ecological Calculator with the actual displaced population, the source province, and the disaster type — and runs a full multi-system ecological impact assessment grounded in real science:

- Urban Heat Island intensity: **Oke (1982)** formula
- Habitat and land-use conversion: **Statistics Canada land coefficients**
- Carbon footprint displacement: **NIR (National Inventory Report)** provincial per-capita data
- Species corridor fragmentation: **SLOSS (Single Large or Several Small)** biodiversity theory
- Watershed stress: provincial baseline water stress indices

The output: CO₂ impact, forest and wetland hectares converted, watershed stress level, biodiversity pressure index, urban heat island delta, and a ranked set of policy interventions — all for the real destination province.

### The Bridge

This is what closes the gap. When a disaster resolves in CityFlow and displacement peaks, a bridge card appears showing:

> *"[N] people displaced from [Source Province] to [Destination Province] — Model the ecological chain reaction →"*

One click navigates to the Ecological Model with source province, destination province, population, and reason pre-filled. The model auto-runs. The visual story becomes quantitative evidence.

## The Asymmetry That Makes This Urgent

A wildfire displaces 50,000 people in **3 days**.

The ecological damage at the receiving city takes **30 years** to become visible.

By the time anyone notices — the watershed stress, the habitat loss, the carbon footprint shift — it is too late to plan, too late to invest, too late to reverse.

Chain Reaction closes that gap. A 30-year ecological consequence made visible in 60 seconds.

## Hackathon Theme Alignment

This project addresses **Earth, Water, and Air** simultaneously — not as separate domains, but as the interconnected systems they actually are:

- **Earth**: land-use conversion, habitat fragmentation, urban sprawl from population concentration
- **Water**: groundwater depletion, watershed stress, waste contamination of water systems
- **Air**: carbon footprint shift, urban heat island intensification, construction and traffic emissions

The challenge statement says: *"Earth's systems are interconnected across water, earth, air, and fire, where changes in one can impact all."*

That is exactly what population displacement does. Chain Reaction makes that interconnection measurable.
